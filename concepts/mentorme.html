Why three categories: income, commitment, reward

Income is the recurring driver — asked as an annual figure and revisited every year, since it's what unlocks everything else. Commitment covers the ongoing costs and routines that come with things like children or housing. Reward & enrolment is where Priority Banking, credit cards and schemes like Baby Bonus live — moments where enrolling now pays off. Every recommendation_rules row is tagged with exactly one of the three.

Why consent_log exists on its own

You asked to store mobile numbers and emails for marketing — under Singapore's PDPA that requires clear, provable opt-in. A boolean on users can't show what someone agreed to or when. Every grant and revoke gets its own timestamped row here instead, so there's a real audit trail if it's ever challenged.

Why reactivation_eligible_at is generated, not typed in

Most Singapore banks won't pay a welcome gift again if you held (or cancelled) the same card in the last 12 months — but the exact cooldown varies by bank and card. cooldown_months is stored per enrolment so reactivation_eligible_at can be computed as cancelled_at + cooldown_months instead of hard-coding one rule for every bank.

Why FD is a contract and ES3 isn't

A fixed deposit's rate is contractual — the bank owes you that number. benchmark_rates for things like the SGX ES3 (STI) ETF or a diversified portfolio are historical averages pulled from public data, not a promise — they can go up, down, or lose money entirely. Every comparison screen using this table needs the same disclaimer: past performance isn't a projection of future returns, and this is general information, not personalised investment advice under MAS rules.

Why card_offers is separate from enrollments

enrollments is what a user actually has. card_offers is what's currently available to sign up for — gift, minimum spend, spend window and a hard valid_until date. These expire on their own schedule (some weekly, some by year), same as benchmark_rates.

Two things make staleness fail safe instead of silent: every read of card_offers filters WHERE valid_until >= now(), so an un-refreshed table just shows fewer offers, never wrong ones — and the "Updated 3d ago" / "Rates checked 12d ago" labels on screens 05 and 06 are computed from MAX(updated_at), not typed in, so staleness is visible to users even if the maintainer falls behind. A monthly check on that same MAX(updated_at) is what should be nagging the maintainer, rather than relying on memory.

Rule logic

What actually triggers each station

The schema only says category is one of three values — these are six of the real rules behind it. Writing them out surfaced two gaps: bank balances aren't collected, so Priority Banking has to be a soft nudge; and a child's age wasn't collected either, so eldest_child_age_band got added to the profile above.

first_card

Get a no-fee first card

career_stage = just_started

Fires once — cleared after the recommendation is actioned in user_recommendations.

twins_commitment

Budget for double costs

children_status → twins_multiples (new)

Same trigger as the reward beside it — this is the interchange, in practice.

twins_reward

Claim Baby Bonus + CDA

children_status → twins_multiples (new)

One trigger, two rows — a commitment and a reward can share a condition.

priority_banking

Check Priority Banking eligibility

income_band ≥ $100k–200k

A soft nudge, not a precise trigger — we don't track actual bank balances, only self-reported income.

school_years

Plan for school-age costs

eldest_child_age_band = school_age

children_status alone can't tell a newborn from a teenager — hence the new field.

career_peak

Build a simple investing habit

career_stage = established AND income_band = $200k+

Income category — nudges toward a habit, not a specific fund (keeps it out of regulated advice).

Calculators

The three formulas doing the work

Every number on screens 05 and 06 comes from one of these — nothing fancier, and nothing that requires bank-account linking to work.

miles_vs_cashback

Miles vs cashback

max( min(spend,cap) × mpd × mile_value, spend × cashback_rate )

Worked example: $2,000 spend → 8,000 miles at ≈2¢ (~$144) beats 1.5% cashback ($30).

fd_monthly_equivalent

Fixed deposit, per month

principal × fd_rate_pct ÷ 12

Worked example: $20,000 at 1.60% p.a. → $26.67/month. Contractual — this one is a promise.

benchmark_monthly_equivalent

ES3 / other investments, per month

principal × annualised_return_pct ÷ 12

Worked example: $20,000 at ~7% p.a. (ES3, 5-yr average) → $116.67/month — historical, not guaranteed.

Sequencing

Next steps

  1. Validate the 6–7 life stages and their trigger rules across income, commitments and reward-enrolment with ~10 real people before writing any code — this content is the actual product.
  2. Build screens 01 and 02 only for v1 (onboarding + the line); add cards, savings and the rest once the line itself feels right.
  3. Write the PDPA consent copy before the marketing toggle ships, not after.
  4. Get real, current numbers for benchmark_rates before showing screen 06 to anyone — the ES3 and portfolio figures here are illustrative, not verified as of today.
  5. Set a refresh cadence for card_offers — the three shown on screen 05 are real July 2026 promotions, but two of them expire within weeks; without a recurring update process this list goes stale fast.
  6. Have someone check the "Grow Your Money" wording against MAS's advice/comparison boundary before launch — informational and comparison content is fine, personalised recommendations to buy aren't, without a licence.
