How to choose an NEA-certified pest control operator

Almost every operator's website says "NEA-certified" and shows a wall of five-star reviews. Here's what that claim actually means, what it doesn't, and what to check yourself before you sign anything — whether you found the vendor through us or elsewhere.

The real credential

What NEA certification actually is

The National Environment Agency regulates Singapore's pest control industry and issues the NEA Vector Control Operator Certificate. It's a real, checkable credential — not a marketing badge an operator can just print on their homepage.

What it covers

  • Technicians are trained and certified in approved application methods
  • Only NEA-approved equipment and chemicals may be used — baiting, misting, soil treatment
  • The company itself, not just an individual technician, is expected to hold current certification

What it doesn't guarantee

  • It's not a quality or price guarantee — certification is a floor, not a ranking
  • It doesn't cover every claim an operator makes about warranties, response times, or "eco-friendly" methods — verify those separately
  • Certification can lapse — always ask for current status, not a certificate photo from three years ago
Before you sign

Five questions worth asking any operator

  • "Can you confirm your current NEA Vector Control Operator certification?" — a legitimate operator answers this without hesitation.
  • "What exactly does each visit cover, and how many visits per month?" — this is where the "$50/month" plans that turn out to be quarterly visits billed at $150+ get caught.
  • "What's your re-treatment policy if the pest comes back?" — a real warranty, not a vague assurance.
  • "What products/methods do you use, and are they safe for [my kitchen / my kids / my pets]?" — a certified operator can name the product family and application method.
  • "Can I get the scope and price in writing before you start?" — especially important for termite jobs, which are usually priced only after inspection.
