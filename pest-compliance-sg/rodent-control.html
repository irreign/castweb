Rodent control

Rats and mice near drains, chutes and food storage are a compliance issue as much as a nuisance — a required part of every SFA contract and a standard line item in MCST common-area programmes.

What treatment usually involves

  • Bait station placement at drains, chutes, roof voids and perimeter points
  • Sealing and proofing recommendations for entry points
  • Regular monitoring visits — bait uptake is checked and replenished on schedule
  • For MCST estates: bait stations are typically mapped across common areas as part of the recurring contract

FAQ

Common questions

Are bait stations safe for a household with kids or pets?
NEA-certified operators use tamper-resistant, lockable bait stations designed to keep the bait inaccessible to children and pets. Ask your matched partner to confirm placement around any specific household risk.
Why do I keep seeing rats even after treatment?
Rodent control is rarely a one-visit fix — it usually needs sustained monitoring and, critically, sealing off the entry points that keep re-introducing them. A single treatment without proofing tends to relapse.
Is rodent control part of my MCST's existing contract?
Almost always, yes — it's bundled into the standard common-area pest programme. If you're not sure what's covered, see our MCST pest control page.
