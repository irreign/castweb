Mosquito control & NEA warning letters

Mosquito breeding in Singapore isn't just a nuisance — it's enforced. NEA can issue a warning letter (and, for repeat or unresolved breeding, a fine) under the Destruction of Disease Bearing Insects Act. If you've received one, this has a deadline attached — tell us and we'll route it as urgent.

What a mosquito control visit covers

  • Inspection for stagnant water and breeding sites — flowerpot trays, gully traps, roof gutters, discarded containers
  • Fogging or larviciding of common areas and identified breeding points
  • For MCST estates: usually a monthly recurring visit, distinct from general pest control
  • For NEA notice cases: documentation of remediation, useful if you need to demonstrate compliance

FAQ

Common questions

What happens if I ignore an NEA breeding notice?
Unresolved or repeat breeding can escalate to enforcement action, including fines, under the Destruction of Disease Bearing Insects Act. Addressing the breeding source and documenting it promptly is the straightforward way to close this out.
Is mosquito control part of my MCST's pest contract, or separate?
It's often billed and scheduled as a distinct monthly line item from general pest control, even when procured from the same vendor — worth confirming explicitly in any tender, as flagged on our MCST pest control page.
Can I just clear the stagnant water myself?
For an obvious, isolated source (a flowerpot tray, a blocked gutter), yes — clearing it yourself may resolve the issue. A professional visit is worth it when the source isn't obvious, or when you need documented remediation for an NEA notice.
