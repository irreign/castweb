Is your cooling tower actually compliant — or just installed?

NEA requires every registered cooling tower in Singapore to be cleaned every 6 months and water-tested for Legionella every 3 months. If it lapses, the fine and the liability sit with the building owner or MCST — not the contractor you hired to do it.

What NEA actually requires

Under the Environmental Public Health (Cooling Towers and Water Fountains) Regulations, every registered cooling tower system in Singapore has a fixed compliance cycle. This isn't a recommendation — it's a registered obligation tied to your building.

RequirementFrequency
Cleaning & disinfectionAt least once every 6 months
Standard plate count water testAt least once a month
Legionella bacteria count (LBC) testAt least once every 3 months, submitted to NEA
Registration as Aerosol Generating System (AGS)One-time, per cooling tower system

Source: NEA Environmental Public Health (Cooling Towers and Water Fountains) Regulations and NEA's published guidelines on cleaning & disinfection of cooling towers.

The part most facilities teams miss: appointing a contractor to clean and test your cooling tower does not transfer the compliance liability. If your contractor misses a cycle or a test result comes back non-compliant, NEA's enforcement action is against the building owner/MCST — first offence up to S$5,000, repeat offences up to S$10,000. You are responsible for knowing your own compliance status, not just trusting that someone else is handling it.

Common questions

We already have an ACMV contractor servicing the chiller plant — isn't this covered?

Sometimes, but not always. General ACMV/chiller maintenance contracts don't automatically include NEA's specific Legionella water-testing schedule and result submission. Ask your current contractor directly whether they are submitting your Legionella Bacteria Count results to NEA on the required quarterly cycle — many facilities discover a gap here only after an enforcement notice.

What happens if we fail a Legionella test?

You'll need immediate remedial cleaning and disinfection, followed by re-testing, while remaining exposed to enforcement action until the result clears. Speed matters here more than in almost any other building compliance area, because of the health risk Legionella bacteria pose.

We just took over facilities management for a building — how do we check our current status?

Start by requesting the building's AGS registration number and the last 12 months of cleaning and water-test records from the previous FM team or owner. If those records don't exist or are incomplete, treat it as a live compliance gap, not a paperwork issue.
